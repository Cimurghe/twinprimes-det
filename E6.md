# E6: a two-scale sign dichotomy for twin-prime parity

Author: Roy Sulaiman. Timestamp note, 24 Nov 2026. Developed with AI assistance (Claude; ChatGPT for the round-18 reduction, credited below). Nothing here is claimed to be a proof of the twin prime conjecture.

## Notation

$\lambda(n)=(-1)^{\Omega(n)}$, $s(m)=\lambda(m)\lambda(m+2)$. Say $n$ is $z$-rough if it has no prime factor $<z$.

Selberg's parity fake puts all the mass of rough pairs on $s=-1$. A twin prime pair has $s=+1$.

## The identity

For $t,k\ge1$ put $u=kt+1$, $v=k(t+2)+1$, $N=tv$. Then
$$N+2=(t+2)\,u,\qquad s(N)=s(t)\cdot\lambda(u)\lambda(v).$$

**Dichotomy.** If $\lambda(u)\lambda(v)=-1$, then exactly one of the pairs $(t,t+2)$ and $(N,N+2)$ has $s=+1$. Every prime factor of $N(N+2)$ divides $t$, $t+2$, $u$ or $v$, so the winning pair is rough whenever those four factors are. You never need to know which pair wins. The parameter $k$ is a smooth auxiliary variable.

## Two-world numerics

The key input is $\lambda(u)\lambda(v)=-1$.
- **True world:** it fires on $\approx 50\%$ of rough configurations, flat from $z=2$ to $z=80$ (25M configurations at $z=2$).
- **Multiplicative fakes:** under the substitutions $\lambda\to\chi_{-4}$ and $\lambda\to\chi_{-3}$ it fires on 0%. On 3-rough configurations, $k$ is forced even, so $\chi_{-4}(u)=\chi_{-4}(v)$ (proved in `lean/E6Core.lean`).

## Reduction of Stage C to one inequality (round 18; reduction written by ChatGPT, checked here)

Let $A_z(k,T)$ count $T<t\le2T$ with all four factors $z$-rough. Let $B_z(k,T)$ be the same sum weighted by $\lambda(u)\lambda(v)$.

1. **Elementary sieve.** For $z=T^{\delta_0}$ with $\delta_0=1/12360$, $A_z(k,T)\ge c_*T/(\log T)^{100}$, uniformly in $6\mid k$.
2. **Counting.** A flip exists $\iff B_z(k,T)<A_z(k,T)$.
3. **Size control.** If $B_z(k,T)<A_z(k,T)$ holds for arbitrarily large $T$ with $k\le CT^B$, then there are infinitely many $m$ with $m$ and $m+2$ both $m^\eta$-rough and $s(m)=+1$, for every $\eta<\delta_0/(B+2)$.

The conclusion of step 3 is a statement the parity fake forbids. A pure sieve cannot prove $B<A$, since the weight $(1+b)\cdot\mathrm{mask}$ has the same sieve statistics.

**The single missing input:** a Bombieri–Vinogradov-type version of two-point Chowla. That means equidistribution of $\lambda(kt+1)\lambda(kt+2k+1)$ in progressions $t\equiv r\ (\mathrm{mod}\ d)$, uniformly for $d\le T^\theta$, with a log-power saving beating sieve dimension 4. The averaged-over-$k$ form (with one common exceptional set of $k$) is preferred.

For fixed coefficients, ordinary two-point correlations with a log saving are claimed in OpenAI, "Ordinary two-point correlations of multiplicative functions" (preprint, 24 Sep 2026, Thm 1.1). That theorem asserts no uniformity for growing coefficients. The gap is exactly that uniformity.

**What is not claimed.** For fixed $z$, or with $k$ allowed beyond every polynomial in $t$, flips exist unconditionally (Teräväinen; Green–Tao's Chen primes). Those versions are vacuous for parity: the winning pair is then only polylog-rough relative to its own size.
